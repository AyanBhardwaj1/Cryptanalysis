# Eighty-six doublets redirect the Liber Primus search

The 56 unsolved Liber Primus pages (LP2 pages 0–55, 12,956 runes) remain completely unread after twelve years. No credible partial solve has been published, and every 2026 lab ledger states that nothing in LP2 is solved ([pnotisdev TRIED.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/TRIED.md); [cicada-ioc](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc)). What has changed is that the space of possible ciphers has narrowed sharply. One measurement does most of the narrowing: the pages contain **86 adjacent identical runes where about 446 are expected (0.66% against 3.45%, z ≈ −17)**. A simple bound shows that no additive or affine cipher whose key is independent of the plaintext can push the rate below about 1.3–1.8% on English-like text. That rules out, as a class, the families the community has swept for a decade: plain Vigenère and Beaufort, running keys from books, prime and totient streams, OEIS sequences and PRNG pads. Those sweeps total tens of billions of decodes. The surviving mechanisms all look back at the previous ciphertext rune. They are a chain cipher, or a key that is re-drawn about 81% of the time when it would repeat the previous rune, possibly combined with inserted filler runes that would explain an anomalous word-length distribution. Almost none of the key material that was "excluded" under plain constructions has been re-tested inside these mechanisms. The lab's best next move is therefore to wrap its existing key library in anti-repeat, chain and filler-aware decoders. Every candidate mechanism should first be filtered by whether its forward model reproduces the doublet fingerprint, and every decoder should be validated on solved pages re-encrypted under that mechanism. Claude should orchestrate, not do the arithmetic.

## Nine solved sections share one house style that LP2 breaks

All solved material dates from 2014 and uses four simple constructions over the 29-rune Gematria Primus. The constructions are plain Gematria, Atbash (with and without a shift of 3), Vigenère, and a stream that subtracts φ(pₙ) = pₙ − 1 over the primes ([rtkd/iddqd keys](https://github.com/rtkd/iddqd/blob/f2267b0c2f1e1d2662806b90b9c3a2953b3a7023/liber-primus__keys/liber-primus__keys.txt); [cicada-ioc SOURCES.md](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc/blob/main/SOURCES.md)). Every keyed section works additively mod 29 in Gematria index order (F=0 … EA=28) and decrypts as p = c − k. Each also obeys the same **interrupter rule: a plaintext F is written as ᚠ unencrypted and does not advance the key**. The interrupter positions cannot be derived from the ciphertext. Solvers found them by trial or by crib; LiberPrayground encodes them as nth-occurrence lists such as `INTERRUPT_POS=[4,5,6,7,10,11,14,18,20,21,25]` for the Welcome section ([LiberPrayground solver.py](https://github.com/relikd/LiberPrayground/blob/master/solver.py)).

| Section | Rule | Key |
|---|---|---|
| A Warning | Atbash, p = 28 − c | — |
| Welcome | Vigenère + F interrupter | DIVINITY (23 10 1 10 9 10 16 26) |
| A Koan / An Instruction | shift 3 then Atbash | — |
| A Koan (circumference) | Vigenère + F interrupter | FIRFUMFERENFE (CIRCUMFERENCE with C→F) |
| An End (LP2 p56) | subtract φ(pₙ) + F interrupter | prime stream |
| Wisdom, Loss of Divinity, Instruction, Parable | plain | — |

The keys follow a recognisable pattern: either a word from the book's own text with a spelling twist, or a "sacred" number stream. The plaintext says outright "THE PRIMES ARE SACRED. THE TOTIENT FUNCTION IS SACRED. ALL THINGS SHOULD BE ENCRYPTED" ([LiberPrayground pages](https://github.com/relikd/LiberPrayground/tree/master/pages)). The book also contains two magic squares written in Gematria sums (magic constants 1033 and 3301), and An End names a deep-web page by its 512-bit hash ([scream314 2014.md](https://github.com/scream314/cicada3301/blob/master/2014.md)). That page has never been found. Leo-Y-Zhang matched the hash against 697,000 candidates and pnotisdev against 124,017 archived onion captures, with no hit ([Leo-Y-Zhang](https://github.com/Leo-Y-Zhang/LiberPrimusAnalysis); [pnotisdev TRIED.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/TRIED.md)). Cicada's one post-2014 signed hint, from January 2016, reads: "Liber Primus is the way. Its words are the map, their meaning is the road, and **their numbers are the direction**". Both later signed messages verify against key 7A35090F and carry no key material ([scream314 2016.md](https://github.com/scream314/cicada3301/blob/master/2016.md); [pnotisdev FINDINGS.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/FINDINGS.md)).

The critical point for the lab is that **the solved ciphertexts show no doublet deficit**. Their doublet rates sit at the uniform rate: 1.8–3.1% for Welcome, Warning, the Koans and An End, against 2.59% for the solved plaintext itself. This lab measured those figures on the relikd transcription; cicada-ioc independently reports the solved pages at 0.86 of uniform against LP2's 0.19 ([cicada-ioc STATUS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc/main/STATUS.md)). So **LP2 does not use the solved pages' single-layer additive scheme with literal-F interrupters**, and pnotisdev reached the same conclusion independently. The house style still matters as a prior on key material (book words, primes, totient, Gematria sums). It is not a prior on the mechanism.

## The doublet floor rules out every plaintext-independent additive key

LP2 is otherwise as flat as a one-time pad. Normalised IoC per section runs 0.981–1.004. The unigram χ² against uniform gives p = 0.55. Periodic IoC peaks no higher than 1.09 for any period up to 40. Repeated 4-, 5- and 6-grams occur at random-text rates, and **no word of four or more runes repeats anywhere** ([Dukotah](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301); [Leo-Y-Zhang](https://github.com/Leo-Y-Zhang/LiberPrimusAnalysis); local measurements on the [relikd transcription](https://github.com/relikd/LiberPrayground)). Flatness alone excludes simple, homophonic and monoalphabetic substitution, per-word keys and periodic keys shorter than a page. It cannot tell running key, OTP, PRNG and feedback ciphers apart, because each of them produces IoC ≈ 1 by itself.

The doublet deficit does tell them apart. Three or more independent 2026 labs report it at the same figure, and the Uncovering Cicada wiki recorded it years earlier as "the only solid clue towards cipher/key found in runes" ([cicada-ioc README §11](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc); [Leo-Y-Zhang](https://github.com/Leo-Y-Zhang/LiberPrimusAnalysis); [Dukotah](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301)). The structure of the deficit is very specific. It is uniform across sections (heterogeneity p = 0.63) and across pages (p = 0.31; the 16 doublet-free pages match the ~15 that Poisson noise predicts). It is the same inside words (0.62%), across word separators (0.79%) and across line breaks (0.68%). **Only lag 1 is affected**: coincidence rates at lags 2–7 sit at the 3.45% baseline. The step Δ = c[i] − c[i−1] is flat on 1..28 and independent of the preceding rune. No triple runs occur. These figures come from the lab's local measurements; cicada-ioc reports the same within-word/across-separator split as 0.0062 against 0.0080 ([cicada-ioc README §4](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc)). The suppression therefore runs continuously through word, line and page boundaries, as if the encryptor carried one "previous ciphertext rune" register across the whole book.

The decisive argument is a lower bound derived in this lab's notes. For c = p + k, a doublet occurs exactly when Δk = −Δp. So P(doublet) ≥ min_d P(Δp = d) for **any** key-difference distribution independent of the plaintext, whether non-uniform, non-stationary, PRNG or book-derived. Beaufort and fixed-multiplier affine ciphers obey the same bound. The floor is 1.80% for English prose and 1.31% for the solved LP plaintext, and the observed 0.66% is far below both. Simulation agrees. A random Vigenère key gives 3.51%. A key that **never repeats its previous shift gives 3.44%, i.e. no suppression at all**. An English running key gives 3.95%, the totient-prime stream 3.21%, and ciphertext autokey with a secondary key 3.43%. Only a chain cipher (c[i] = c[i−1] + nonzero step) or a skip-key-if-c[i]=c[i−1] rule suppresses doublets, and both suppress them completely, to 0.00% ([relikd transcription](https://github.com/relikd/LiberPrayground), local simulation). Pure ciphertext autokey without a key reproduces the deficit, but it fails in another way: its Δ histogram reproduces the plaintext letter distribution, which is grossly non-flat.

That leaves a gap between a hard rule (0 doublets) and the observed 86. Closing it with transcription error would need a ~9.6% glyph error rate, which contradicts the reported 99.7% held-out read accuracy ([cicada-ioc STATUS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc/main/STATUS.md)). cicada-ioc scored 20 constructions on four statistics at once (doublet count, lag-1 difference-class χ², unigram χ² and IoC), with 200 replicates each. **Only "uniform key re-drawn when it would repeat, ~81% of the time" fit all four (87 ± 10 doublets)**. Constructions that match the count but fail elsewhere were eliminated: "+1 when a doublet would form" (lag-1 χ² 299 against 42), ciphertext feedback tuned so that a doublet means EA (χ² 9,629), and Vigenère that steps its key on a doublet (15 doublets) ([cicada-ioc README §5](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc)). Dukotah fits p_keep ≈ 0.18 and calls the mechanism machine-applied. He also warns that a soft anti-repeat rewrite of the output can set the doublet rate by itself, so **the deficit constrains the mechanism but not the key type** ([Dukotah](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301)). Nobody has explained why about 19% of doublets would survive a deterministic program. The live candidates are a probabilistic rule; a rule checked in a different representation (Latin letters, or the rune two places back); or a rule gated by a secondary condition that holds ~81% of the time. This is an open and cheaply testable question.

The lab's own `src/lplab/stats.py` docstring attributes the anomaly to "a key that never repeats a shift of 0 with respect to the previous character". The simulation above refutes this. The constraint applies to the **ciphertext** (c[i] ≠ c[i−1] about 81% of the time), not to the key, and the docstring should be corrected before it misleads the orchestrator.

### Secondary anomalies are weak but shape the mechanism

The second robust anomaly concerns word lengths. The separators are in clear, but **2-rune words make up 15.5% of LP2 against 22–24% in English and the solved plaintext**, and long words are over-represented (χ² ≈ 195–213, p < 1e−37 locally). cicada-ioc reports the same deficit independently (15.9%, χ² 183) and fits a filler rate of about 0.40 null runes per word ([cicada-ioc STATUS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc/main/STATUS.md)); pnotisdev fits one extra rune in 42–45% of words ([pnotisdev FINDINGS.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/FINDINGS.md)). This statistic depends on transcription conventions. If line wraps are treated as word breaks, 2-rune words rise to 18.8%. Single-rune words decrypt to A or I in 27 of 27 solved cases, while LP2's 83 one-rune words spread across many runes (T 19, U 18, EA 17 …). This shows the key varies per position rather than per word, and it gives the lab a free verifier: any real decryption must turn nearly all of those 83 words into A or I.

The remaining signals are marginal and partly contested. They are a lag-6 relation inside words, c′ = 24 − 2c mod 29 (80 or 77 hits against ~44 expected, z ≈ +5); a lag-5 equality excess that cicada-ioc keeps (102 against 71) and pnotisdev drops as a selection effect; an excess of S pairs (11 against 3.5, family-wise p 0.085); and a single repeated 6-gram, D-J-U-B-E-I. That 6-gram occurs once mid-corpus and again as the book's last six runes, with roughly a 13% chance of arising by accident ([pnotisdev FINDINGS.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/FINDINGS.md); [cicada-ioc STATUS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc/main/STATUS.md)). Their family-wise p-values sit near 0.01 after very large searches, so each deserves one pre-registered replication rather than heavy investment. One more inference from the lab's measurements: ᚠ makes up 3.54% of LP2, essentially 1/29. Unencrypted F interrupters at English frequency would inflate it to roughly 5–6%, which argues against the solved pages' literal-F rule in LP2 (local measurement and inference).

## Billions of null decodes mostly tested the wrong construction

The public negative record is large, and a new lab should diff against it before sweeping anything. pnotisdev alone reports a 1,447-stream number library (409 M decodes, 5.7 bn with all multipliers), linear recurrences and polynomials of order ≤ 3 (9.2 bn), 199,088 OEIS sequences, hash streams of all 12,944 solved-text words and phrases (8.1 bn), ten PRNGs over all 2³² seeds and 19.2 M passphrases. It also covered autokeys to lag 8, periodic keys, sign-alternation templates, clocked key pointers, 4,608 small state machines, key restarts at every structural boundary, and 17 books in 6 languages as running keys ([pnotisdev TRIED.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/TRIED.md)).

Dukotah swept about 14.5×10⁹ public-pad offsets (Bitcoin block heights, the NIST Beacon, RANDOM.ORG, RAND digits) and about 200 key texts under skip and rewrite constructions ([Dukotah](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301)). cicada-ioc ran about 1.24 B pre-registered decoder tests with planted controls and closed 46 of 76 lanes. Its depth-two test also rules out aligned key reuse between pages for any offset within ±200 runes ([cicada-ioc](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc)). LiberPrayground's InterruptDB ran about 1.36×10¹⁰ operations searching interrupter sets ([LiberPrayground](https://github.com/relikd/LiberPrayground)).

The doublet floor reframes most of this work. Any plaintext-independent additive family produces 430+ doublets, so it can be rejected **before decoding**. Running such families "plain" was always going to fail, and it is the most-repeated dead end in the record. The exclusions are also construction-specific: a key stream excluded under plain Vigenère is **not** excluded under Vigenère-plus-soft-redraw, chain encoding or filler insertion. No source consolidates which families were re-tested inside those mechanisms. Leo-Y-Zhang tested 46 integer sequences in chain form, and Dukotah ran his ~200 texts under skip and rewrite. Keyword keys, periodic keys, LP-text running keys and most integer streams appear untested under the soft rule, and so does non-English scoring in the delta domain. Two further blind spots stand out. First, nearly every decoder models a key that may fail to advance, but none has "state for a page rune that consumed no key". cicada-ioc measures **the plain decoder's power at 0.00 against one filler in ten** ([cicada-ioc README §11](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc)). Second, most scorers were English-only: Dukotah reports power of 0.33 for Latin and 0.00 for Welsh ([Dukotah](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301)).

The labs themselves leave the queue partly open. pnotisdev built but never ran a word-edge insertion campaign (~300 CPU-hours over 82 windows; the one-window pilot was null). It also queued "pointer direction by the words' numbers" under 18 conventions after only a pilot, and it never downloaded Hofstadter's GEB, the 2014 onion-5 book-code source ([pnotisdev TRIED.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/TRIED.md); [pnotisdev SOURCES.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/SOURCES.md)). The labs are also honest about the ceiling. cicada-ioc puts 40% on "key material not public" and 30% on "an unenumerated process" ([cicada-ioc README §0](https://github.com/RiskAverseTech/cicada-ioc)); pnotisdev estimates a ~50% chance that the key cannot be recovered from the book alone. All of these ledgers are self-published, AI-assisted and unreplicated, so the lab should treat their negatives as strong hints rather than proofs.

## Ten experiments, ranked by information per CPU-hour

The ranking below weighs three things: fit to the measured constraints, compute cost, and how much prior work left each hypothesis open. Each experiment needs a positive control: re-encrypt the solved pages (~3,000 runes) under the candidate mechanism and prove that the decoder recovers them at LP2's length.

| # | Experiment | Why now | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Forward-model gate**: simulate each candidate mechanism on rune-English and require ~86 ± 10 doublets, ~23–26 across word gaps, flat lag-1 Δ χ², flat unigrams and IoC ≈ 1 | Kills dead families before any decoding; reuses the cicada-ioc four-statistic test | Minutes |
| 2 | **Delta-stream reformulation**: s[i] = c[i] − c[i−1] in Z28 and Z29, both signs; run the full existing battery on s (DIVINITY, FIRFUMFERENFE, φ(prime), affine, periodic ≤ 100, LP-text and Cicada-text running keys) | Chain ciphers are the cleanest fit to the hard constraint; reuses all existing code | Low |
| 3 | **Soft-redraw decoder** for every stream in the library (see engineering section); fit p_keep; test deterministic triggers that would give the 19% leak (rule checked in Latin letters, at lag 2, or only when k ≠ 0) | Only construction that fits all four statistics; most key families untested under it | Low–medium |
| 4 | **Deconvolution closure**: DFT-deconvolve the observed Δc histogram by Δp histograms of English, Latin, Old English and Welsh rune corpora, also per word-position class | Formally closes the independent-key class including non-English loopholes | Minutes |
| 5 | **Filler-aware decoding**: deletion bit-vector or HMM state for "rune consumed no key", λ ≈ 0.40 per word, length-normalised scoring; check whether doublet-avoidance insertions could themselves produce the excess length | Explains the word-length anomaly; existing decoders have zero power here | Medium (~300 CPU-h for word-edge campaign) |
| 6 | **"Numbers are the direction" pointers**: word Gematria sums set key-pointer step, sign (Vigenère/Beaufort) or direction, combined with soft-redraw | Most specific unexhausted signed hint; only piloted | Medium |
| 7 | **Replicate secondary anomalies** (lag-6 relation, lag-5 equality, S pairs) with pre-registered definitions; if real, test feedback taps such as c[i] = p[i] + k[i] + c[i−5] | Would add a second mechanism constraint | Low |
| 8 | **D-J-U-B-E-I depth test** at offset 6,395 under raw, Δ and skip-adjusted alignments | Cheap; low prior (~13% chance repeat) | Minutes |
| 9 | **PRNG with rejection loop consuming extra outputs** (Python 2.7 MT, PHP mt_rand, bash $RANDOM, glibc rand, Java LCG) over 2³² seeds | Plain-stream seed sweeps miss the realignment; Dukotah's exact/keyskip1 sweep stopped at 44% | High |
| 10 | **Non-English re-scoring** of everything from experiments 2, 3, 5 and 6 with Latin, Old English and Welsh rune models; add GEB as key/pointer source | Prior scorers had ~0 power off English | Marginal once 2–6 exist |

The order is deliberate. Experiments 1 and 4 are analytic and cost minutes, yet they decide which mechanism families deserve billions of decodes. Experiments 2 and 3 have the highest prior per unit cost, because they reuse the whole key library inside the only mechanisms that pass the gate. Experiment 9 is well defined but expensive, and it should run only after cheaper wrappers have been shown to recover planted PRNG keys.

## Engineering: exploit structure, then make the inner loop branch-free

The practitioners with record results all converge on a single architecture: Lasry, Kopal and Wacker; CrypTool 2; and AZdecrypt, the solver used to break Zodiac Z340. An outer loop enumerates structural hypotheses. A fast inner solver handles each one, using hill climbing or simulated annealing with many restarts, scoring that is selective and stage-appropriate, and divide-and-conquer wherever the keyspace factors ([Lasry thesis, Kassel 2018](https://www.uni-kassel.de/upress/online/OpenAccess/978-3-7376-0458-1.OpenAccess.pdf); [Kopal, HistoCrypt 2019](https://ep.liu.se/ecp/158/012/ecp19158012.pdf)). Z340 was broken by pushing **655,088 transposition variants** through AZdecrypt's inner homophonic solver and ranking the results ([Oranchak, Blake, Van Eycke 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17350)). Lasry's double-transposition attack hill-climbs one key with IDP, a specialised statistic that is monotonic for partly correct keys, before attacking the other ([Lasry et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263286752_Solving_the_Double_Transposition_Challenge_with_a_Divide-and-Conquer_Approach)). No source reports genetic algorithms beating SA or hill climbing on any classical cipher, so GAs are low priority. LP2 maps onto this pattern directly. The outer loop is mechanism × key family × alignment rule × plaintext language. The inner loop is an exact or near-exact decode, scored by n-grams.

Scoring should use dense log-likelihood n-gram tables built from a **large English corpus transliterated into runes with the same greedy digraph rules (TH, EO, NG, EA …) used on the solved pages**. The solved LP text is far too small to estimate 4-grams. A 29-symbol quadgram table has 29⁴ = 707,281 entries (≈1.4 MB as int16) and fits in cache, indexed by a base-29 rolling index. Pentagrams (20.5 M entries) are feasible but cache-hostile. Unseen n-grams need a floor probability ([Naskręcki ch. 9](https://bnaskrecki.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/crypto/book/part3_polygraphic_ciphers/ch09_automated_cryptanalysis.html)), and scores must be normalised per n-gram, and ideally z-scored against random rune text of equal length, so that null-deletion hypotheses cannot win simply by deleting runes. The known word boundaries are the largest structural advantage available. Treat the separator as a 30th symbol in the n-gram model to capture word-initial and word-final statistics. Add a dictionary-hit term over rune-transliterated words, and add the A/I check on one-rune words and the prime-clause-sum check as verifiers: 36 of 76 solved clauses have prime Gematria sums, z = 7.9 ([pnotisdev FINDINGS.md](https://github.com/pnotisdev/cicada3301/blob/15200c67abee106913ba01ff504c5b6e88d16cc7/docs/FINDINGS.md)). Pattern-word and dictionary methods exploit word boundaries in exactly this way for substitution ciphers ([ALICE related work](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07282)).

Throughput comes from incremental scoring and early abort. AZdecrypt solves up to **~200 homophonic ciphers per second on an 8-core CPU** at a 99%+ solve rate. It aborts n-gram scoring as soon as a candidate cannot beat the current score, a trick that pays off most with sparse high-order n-grams ([arXiv:2403.17350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17350); [AZdecrypt changelog](https://forum.zodiackillerciphers.com/community/zodiac-cipher-mailings-discussion/azdecrypt-1-19b/paged/6/)). In numba, write `@njit(cache=True)` kernels over uint8 ciphertext with a separate boundary mask, and keep a per-position n-gram score array so a key change re-scores only the affected positions. Use `prange` across restarts or hypotheses, and for SA draw the acceptance threshold before scoring so that early abort applies. For a key stream from a library (streams, books, seeds), score a short prefix (~60–100 runes) first and decode fully only the survivors. This prefix screen is what makes 2³²-seed sweeps tractable: by rough estimate, 2³² × ~80 runes ≈ 3×10¹¹ rune-operations, or hours on a multicore box (unsourced order-of-magnitude estimate). For periodic keys of known period, skip metaheuristics entirely and use exact per-column optimisation followed by quadgram coordinate ascent.

The mechanisms the statistics favour turn out to be cheap to decode, and this is the lab's key engineering insight. **A chain cipher costs one subtraction pass** (the Δ transform), after which every existing attack applies unchanged. A **soft-redraw decoder branches only sparsely**. Suppose the encryptor computed c = p + k[j] and advanced to k[j+1] whenever that would equal c[i−1]. When decrypting, a skip at position i is possible only if c[i] − k[j+1] = c[i−1] − k[j]. That coincidence holds at about 1/29 of positions, so a beam of modest width, or a three-state HMM over key-index advance {0, 1, 2}, decodes at near plain-Vigenère speed (an inference from the mechanism's algebra, matching the emission constraint in the lab's notes). The same lattice extends to PRNG keys whose rejection loop consumes extra outputs, and to filler insertion. For filler insertion, add a "consumed no key" state with a per-null prior penalty, so the decoder cannot simply delete its way to English. Use a Griffing-style Viterbi over joint plaintext and key n-grams, or Reddy and Knight's faster word-LM Gibbs sampler, only for blind two-text problems where both streams are natural language and no candidate book is known. Recovery is good near 1,000 characters and poor on short fragments ([Griffing 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220615828_Solving_the_Running_Key_Cipher_with_the_Viterbi_Algorithm); [Reddy & Knight 2012](https://aclanthology.org/P12-2016/)). When a candidate book exists, an O(N_book × L) offset sweep is far stronger.

Claude's role should mirror the 2026 DescryptTool design, in which the LLM selects and parameterises fixed solvers and is barred from free code execution ([Kopal & Megyesi, Cryptologia 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01611194.2026.2693460)). LLMs fail at the raw arithmetic: on CipherBank, o1 scored **7.25 on Vigenère** and DeepSeek-R1 0.38 ([CipherBank](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.19093)). Blind cipher identification also collapses once metadata is removed ([ACTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26893)). Those results leave a well-defined job for the orchestrator. It generates mechanism and key-source hypotheses, diffs each family against the pnotisdev, cicada-ioc and Dukotah ledgers before enqueueing it, and enforces the forward-model gate and planted controls. It re-ranks only the top 10–1,000 candidates for semantic plausibility, where neural LMs separate "English at a distance" better than n-grams ([arXiv:2010.04746](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.04746)). Every claimed decryption must be re-run deterministically, because LLMs hallucinate confident wrong keys and a neural K4 attempt was found regurgitating its cribs ([TechRxiv](https://www.techrxiv.org/doi/10.36227/techrxiv.175295517.70390739); [kryptos-k4 attempt](https://github.com/mmtmn/kryptos-k4-neural-network-attempt)).

## Conclusion

The field's main failure has been one of construction, not of compute. A decade of keystream hunting tested good key candidates inside a mechanism that the ciphertext had already ruled out at z ≈ −17. The doublet bound converts that history into a usable asset. The key library is largely intact; it simply has to be re-run inside chain, soft-redraw and filler-aware decoders, and those decoders are nearly as cheap as the plain ones. The 19% doublet leak is the most informative unexplained number in the corpus. A deterministic trigger that reproduces it would pin down the program Cicada ran far more tightly than any further key sweep.

The lab should also plan around the realistic ceiling. Two careful ledgers put 40–50% on the key not being recoverable from public material, and An End's hash points to at least one artifact that remains off-book. The highest-value output of this lab may therefore be a defensible closure: a ledger showing that every public key family has been tested under every mechanism that passes the statistical gate, in English and non-English, with planted-control power stated. That would leave a precise, falsifiable specification of what the missing key must look like, and it would turn the next decade's work from a lottery into a search.
